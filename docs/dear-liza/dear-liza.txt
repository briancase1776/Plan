There's a Hole in My Bucket (Dear Liza)
Traditional (public domain); from the German "Wenn der Beltz em Loch
hat" (c. 1700). Best-known recording: Harry Belafonte and Odetta, 1960.

[The verses -- the full loop]

There's a hole in my bucket, dear Liza, dear Liza,
There's a hole in my bucket, dear Liza, a hole.

Then fix it, dear Henry, dear Henry, dear Henry,
Then fix it, dear Henry, dear Henry, fix it.

With what shall I fix it, dear Liza? ... With straw.
But the straw is too long, dear Liza ...
Then cut it, dear Henry ...
With what shall I cut it, dear Liza? ... With an axe.
But the axe is too dull, dear Liza ...
Then sharpen it, dear Henry ...
On what shall I sharpen it, dear Liza? ... On a stone.
But the stone is too dry, dear Liza ...
Then wet it, dear Henry ...
With what shall I wet it, dear Liza? ... With water.
In what shall I fetch it, dear Liza? ... In a bucket.

But there's a hole in my bucket, dear Liza, dear Liza,
There's a hole in my bucket, dear Liza, a hole.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE GRAPH READING
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The song is a dependency graph, sung. Write it as needs edges:

  fix-bucket  needs  straw
  straw       needs  axe        (too long -- cut it)
  axe         needs  stone      (too dull -- sharpen it)
  stone       needs  water      (too dry -- wet it)
  water       needs  bucket     (fetch it in something)
  bucket      needs  fix-bucket

Six edges, each one locally reasonable -- every single verse is a
sensible answer to a sensible question. And the walk arrives back at
the broken node. That is the whole anatomy of a circular dependency:
no individual edge is wrong, the LOOP is wrong, and you cannot see the
loop from inside any one verse.

Third Rock (docs/third_rock/) is this song's sibling. Third Rock is
the chain that cascades OUTWARD -- cause and effect ripping through a
town, every link explicable, the pattern visible only from far enough
away. Dear Liza is the chain that loops BACK -- cause and effect
politely eating its own tail forever. Same lesson from opposite
directions: individual steps that make perfect local sense, and a
structure you only catch by tracing the whole thing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY IT MATTERS IN THIS PROJECT
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The plan tree (notes/plan/, spec notes/plan-tree.md) computes
everything from needs edges: BLOCKED and the READY frontier are
derived, never stored. That design kills stale markers -- and it has
exactly one silent failure mode, and it is this song.

Put a cycle in the needs graph and:

  - nothing dangles, so the dangling-edge audit stays green;
  - every status is set, so the status audit stays green;
  - no member of the loop can ever settle, so no member ever reaches
    the frontier -- they simply stop appearing in "what's next";
  - and nobody is told, because no rule was broken. The items do not
    fail; they just politely wait forever, verse after verse.

Henry and Liza can sing all night. The bucket never gets fixed.

THE CHECK: plan-graph.js --liza walks the needs graph and names any
loop as the verse it is:

  CYCLE  1 needs 2 needs 3 needs 1  -- a hole in the bucket, dear Liza

It also runs inside --check (the drift audit), so a commit that closes
a loop gets caught at the weld. Clean output is exactly what you would
hope: "no hole in the bucket, dear Liza". super (lineage) edges are
never walked -- provenance cannot block, so provenance cannot loop.

THE AXE: detection is not resolution. A cycle is never fixed by
staring at it or re-running the check; some edge in the loop has to be
CUT, and which one is a judgment call, not a computation. Drop it
(soft cross-ref that never was a dependency), demote it to super
(lineage mis-encoded as blocking), or split an item so the two halves
break the loop. That ruling is Brian's -- the same eye as
/plan-edge-audit, which is where cycle findings land for a verdict.
Claude brings the song sheet; Brian brings the axe.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE RECURSION (of course there is one)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The memory apparatus this project runs on is itself bucket-shaped: the
indexes index the indexes, the plan plans the plan tooling, anti-drew
documents why anti-drew exists. Self-reference is fine -- it is how the
system feeds itself. What separates a healthy loop from a Liza loop is
an edge that settles: somewhere, some step actually finishes and clears
a blocker, instead of handing the pail back around the circle. When a
process in this repo starts to feel like the verses -- every step
sensible, no step concluding -- stop singing and go find which edge to
cut. Check the bucket first; it is usually the bucket.
